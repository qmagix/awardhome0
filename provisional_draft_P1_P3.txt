TITLE OF THE INVENTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SYSTEMS AND METHODS FOR MODERATION-GATED MULTI-PAGE ACHIEVEMENT ARTIFACTS,
DERIVATIVE PUBLICATION THEREOF, AND GUARDIAN-MEDIATED SELECTIVE DISCLOSURE OF
PORTABLE PARTICIPANT IDENTITY PROFILES

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIELD OF THE INVENTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[0001] The invention relates to computer-implemented systems for representing
verified achievement records as interactive, shareable digital artifacts; to
content-moderation architectures that make automated derivative publication of
such artifacts safe by construction; and to consent-mediated identity services
through which a participant (or the guardian of a minor participant) selectively
discloses profile and achievement data to third-party event organizers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BACKGROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[0002] Competitive youth activities (competitive dance being the exemplary
domain herein) generate large volumes of achievement results across many
independent event organizers. Results are published inconsistently, attach to
ambiguous participant names, and are rarely aggregated. Participants — most of
whom are minors — have no durable digital representation of their achievements;
organizers have no reliable way to verify a registrant's identity or competitive
history; and any user-generated enrichment of achievement records (photographs,
messages) raises acute child-safety and moderation problems.

[0003] Existing digital "cards" or share graphics are static compositions: they
are either generated without user content (safe but impersonal) or accept user
content without structural guarantees that unmoderated content cannot reach a
public or derivative surface (personal but unsafe). Automated derivative media
generation (e.g., automatic highlight videos) conventionally requires either
human review of each generated asset or acceptance of publication risk.

[0004] Separately, event registration requires re-entry of the same participant
data (identity, date of birth, guardian contacts, waivers, competitive level)
for every event of every organizer, producing transcription errors, division
misplacement (including deliberate "sandbagging"), and repeated collection of a
minor's personal data by many parties. Generic identity providers do not hold
verifiable competitive-history data and provide no guardian-scoped, field-level
disclosure control suitable for minors.

[0005] There is a need for (a) an achievement artifact whose enriched content is
publish-safe by construction, whose composition adapts per viewer, and which can
serve as the sole trusted frame source for automated derivative media; and (b) a
portable participant identifier through which registration data and verified
history flow to organizers only under revocable, field-scoped, guardian-mediated
consent.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[0006] Part I discloses an achievement-artifact system in which an imported,
provenance-bearing achievement record is rendered as a card object having a
front face and, upon a flip interaction, an ordered stack of back pages. Each
back page is CONDITIONALLY MATERIALIZED: the page exists in the rendered
artifact only when its underlying content item exists and satisfies a
moderation-state condition. Because absent or unapproved content yields an
absent page (never an empty or placeholder page on a public surface), every
public rendering of the artifact is complete and publish-safe by construction.

[0007] Further aspects of Part I include: per-viewer composition of a shared
group artifact from junction-record annotations (each group member's
acknowledgement line stored on the member's own junction row, with the viewing
member's line pinned first); a single-surface WYSIWYG editor in which the same
artifact object renders with gated pages materialized as inline-editable
placeholders only in edit mode; propagation of content and moderation verdicts
across awards derived from the same underlying routine; a role-partitioned
brand-mark fitting workflow in which a third-party organizer's mark enters a
fixed silhouette with operator-only geometric controls and an operator-only
public-display approval bit; automated generation of derivative media (vertical
short-form video, share images) whose every frame source is an
already-moderated page composition, rendering the derivative publish-safe
without per-asset human review; and a server-side, provenance-scoped,
seeded probabilistic reward page occasionally materialized within the artifact's
page stack, with guardian-routed redemption.

[0008] Part II discloses a portable participant identity service in which each
participant holds a lifetime, non-recycled public identifier; a guardian (or
adult participant) creates field-scoped, time-bounded, revocable disclosure
grants to specific organizer grantees; organizers pull only granted fields via
authenticated API calls; every disclosure is audit-logged; and the disclosed
profile may include platform-verified competitive-history attestations (e.g.,
division/level placement evidence) that the identity service derives from its
achievement-record corpus. The artifact system of Part I and the identity
service of Part II interoperate: the verified records underlying artifacts are
the source of the attestations disclosed under Part II grants.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Terminology
-----------

[0010] "Achievement record" means a data record representing a result earned at
an event (exemplarily: award name, placement, category, performance name, event,
season/year, source organization), imported from a third-party source with
provenance metadata (source identifier, scrape/import lineage). "Participant"
means the person(s) credited; in the exemplary embodiment, dancers, many of whom
are minors. "Operator" means the platform administrator (exemplarily a
"superadmin" role). "Owner" means an authenticated user with a verified
ownership claim over a participant profile or organization. "Moderation state"
means a per-content-item status taking values including at least {pending,
approved, rejected}.

PART I — THE MODERATION-GATED MULTI-PAGE ACHIEVEMENT ARTIFACT
=============================================================

I.A. Underlying data model (FIG. 1)
-----------------------------------

[0011] Achievement records are stored in a record store in which multi-member
credits are represented through junction records rather than a single foreign
key: a table award_dancers associates one award with N participant identities,
and a table dancer_studios associates participants with affiliated organizations
(studios). Cross-studio collaborations are represented as distinct pseudo-studio
rows whose members bridge affiliations via dancer_studios. This junction
architecture is load-bearing for the per-viewer composition of section I.D.

[0012] Enrichment content attaches at specific granularities, each row carrying
its own moderation state:

  award_acknowledgements(id, award_id, dancer_id, message TEXT,
      status DEFAULT 'pending', created_by, created_at, updated_at)
      — one acknowledgement line (exemplarily <= 280 characters) per
      (award, participant) pair;

  award_card_photos(id, award_id, dancer_id, photo_url,
      status DEFAULT 'pending', uploaded_by, created_at, updated_at)
      — a per-(award, participant) photograph, so that on a group award each
      family controls only its own member's rendering;

  a per-participant default photograph (dancers.card_photo_*) usable as a
      fallback when a specific award has no approved photo;

  organizations.custom_icons — a JSON field carrying the organizer brand-mark
      configuration of section I.E (logo asset reference, logo_size,
      logo_opacity, logo_x/logo_y position, logo_rotation, colophon_message,
      logo_approved boolean).

[0013] Participant identities carry a lifetime public identifier (a
non-recycled, non-guessable unique_id such as "DNC-<random>-<name-slug>") used
in all public URLs in place of enumerable numeric keys; numeric-key public URLs
intentionally return "not found" without redirect so that the identifier space
cannot be enumerated. The same identifier is the portable identifier of Part II.

I.B. The card object and its back-stack (FIG. 2)
------------------------------------------------

[0014] The rendering engine renders an achievement record as a card object
having a front face (award identity: award name, placement/tier styling,
category, performance name, participant and studio attribution lines, event and
season) and a back reached by a flip interaction (tap/click; CSS 3D transform in
the exemplary web embodiment). In a first ("classic") design the back is a
single certificate face. In the flip-book design disclosed here, the back is an
ordered stack of pages navigated by tap, swipe, arrow controls, pagination dots,
and keyboard arrows, with wrap-around:

  Page 1 — CERTIFICATE: a formal "Presented to <participant>" composition
           derived entirely from the verified record; always present, so the
           first flip always lands on a complete page. A share control lives on
           the back across all pages.
  Page 2 — PHOTO: the (award, participant) photograph if one is approved;
           otherwise the participant's approved default photograph in an
           alternative (medallion) composition; otherwise the page does not
           exist.
  Page 3 — ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS: approved acknowledgement line(s) for the award;
           on group awards, composed per viewer as in section I.D; absent if no
           approved lines exist.
  Page 4 — ORGANIZER COLOPHON: "Presented by" composition showing the source
           organization's fitted brand coin at large scale plus an optional
           organizer-authored tagline; exists only when the brand mark has
           passed the operator approval gate of section I.E.

[0015] All internal dimensions of the card and its pages are expressed in
container-relative units (CSS container queries; cqw units calibrated to a
design width), so the artifact scales like an image across surfaces (full page,
grids, lightboxes, mini modes) without per-surface layout code — a property
relied upon by the derivative-media generator of section I.F, which rasterizes
the same compositions at chosen resolutions.

I.C. Conditional materialization (FIG. 3)
-----------------------------------------

[0016] For each candidate page, the renderer evaluates: (a) does the underlying
content item exist; (b) is its moderation state 'approved'; (c) is the
governing feature flag enabled for this viewer (flags take states off/beta/on,
with a beta cohort and an optional scheduled lazy flip-to-on); and (d) for the
colophon, is the organizer mark operator-approved. Only if all conditions hold
is the page materialized into the artifact; otherwise the page is entirely
absent from the rendered object (no placeholder, no empty state) and the
pagination adapts. Surfaces must pass flags explicitly to the card renderer;
an absent flag is treated as off, so a forgotten integration cannot leak gated
content ("dark by default" wiring).

[0017] Consequences of this construction: (i) every public rendering is
complete — there is no state in which a viewer sees missing, pending, or
rejected content; (ii) any consumer that composes from pages (screenshots,
share images, the video generator) inherits publish-safety without independent
checks; (iii) deployment is decoupled from release — code ships dark and
content-bearing pages appear per audience cohort.

I.D. Per-viewer composition of shared group artifacts (FIG. 4)
--------------------------------------------------------------

[0018] A group award is one record credited to N participants via
award_dancers. Each participant may author one acknowledgement line stored on
their own (award_id, dancer_id) row of award_acknowledgements — the "yearbook
back": one physical trophy, N personal inscriptions. When the acknowledgements
page renders on a given participant's public page, the composition pins that
participant's approved line first, followed by teammates' approved lines. The
same artifact therefore lawfully renders differently per viewing context while
remaining a single record — including in derivative media, where each member's
video variant (section I.F) pins their own line.

[0019] Attribution asymmetry: solo card fronts carry the participant's name;
group card fronts remain roster-free (the certificate back reads "together with
teammates"), so that a shared screenshot never discloses a full minor roster on
the most-shared face.

I.E. Role-partitioned brand-mark fitting ("the coin") (FIG. 7)
--------------------------------------------------------------

[0020] Organizer brand marks appear on artifacts only inside a fixed circular
silhouette (the coin), preserving visual uniformity across mixed-organizer
grids. Control of the fit is partitioned by role: geometric controls that could
break the silhouette (position x/y, rotation, and fine scale) are
operator-only; the organizer owner controls only size within bounds and
opacity; and a public-display approval bit (logo_approved) is operator-only and
defaults OFF. Owner edits to owner-controlled parameters ride the same approval
gate, so no owner action can alter public rendering without operator approval.
The colophon page of section I.B and the organizer tagline ride this same gate.

I.F. Derivative media generation from pre-moderated compositions (FIG. 8)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

[0021] A derivative-media generator produces share images and short-form
vertical video for an artifact by sequencing rasterizations of the artifact's
OWN pages — front face, certificate, photo, acknowledgements, colophon — with
flip/page transitions and audio cues. Frame-source eligibility is exactly the
materialization predicate of section I.C evaluated at generation time: a page
enters the frame sequence only if its content is currently approved. The
generator therefore requires no human review of any generated asset: every
pixel source has already passed moderation, and publish-safety is inherited
structurally ("safe by construction"). In embodiments, the organizer colophon
is the enforced terminal frame (end-card), making organizer branding a
structural property of every derivative asset; and per-viewer variants of a
group award's video are generated by applying the pinning rule of section I.D.
Rendering embodiments include server-side rasterization of the same layout
templates (e.g., headless-browser capture at fixed container width) composed by
a media pipeline, and client-side canvas/WebCodecs composition; a re-acquisition
overlay (QR/sticker) may be composited onto share images.

I.G. Single-surface WYSIWYG editing (FIG. 5)
--------------------------------------------

[0022] The owner's editing surface renders the participant's actual artifacts
with an edit-mode flag. In edit mode ONLY, pages that would materialize if
content existed are materialized as inline-editable placeholders ("Upload this
routine's photo", "Write your thank-you note here"); public rendering never
materializes an empty page. Edits save inline (JSON variants of the same
endpoints); the front face carries a completeness chip (waiting/partial/done);
incomplete-first ordering and filter chips guide completion. The display
artifact and the editing surface are the same object rendered under different
mode flags — eliminating any divergence between what is edited and what is
published. Consent capture (a one-time guardian/owner consent affirmation) is
collected on this surface before photo content is accepted.

I.H. Tiered moderation and propagation (FIG. 6)
-----------------------------------------------

[0023] All owner-supplied text/photo content defaults to 'pending' and reaches
public rendering only at 'approved' (operator queue at an admin console). Text
moderation runs tiered: tier 1 deterministic rules (links, e-mail addresses,
phone numbers, social handles — spam and minor-PII protection — plus
profanity); tier 2 trusted-author bypass (authors with >= K approved items skip
the machine tier); tier 3 a machine-learning moderation classifier. An
operator-selectable moderation mode {manual, assisted, auto} controls whether
machine verdicts queue as hints or auto-approve clean items; classifier failure
never auto-approves (fail-closed). Auto-approved items enter a
trust-but-verify review feed with one-click revocation.

[0024] Same-routine propagation: one routine commonly wins several awards at
one event. Saving a note or photo on one award auto-fills sibling awards
matched on (event_id, performance_name) using insert-or-ignore semantics
(already-filled siblings keep their content; later edits and clears affect only
the edited award). Moderation verdicts propagate identically: one
approve/reject settles every pending copy with identical content from the same
participant. Propagation thus multiplies both authoring effort and moderation
effort by the routine's award count without multiplying risk.

I.I. Provenance-scoped surprise reward page (FIG. 9)
----------------------------------------------------

[0025] In embodiments, a reward subsystem occasionally materializes a
transient additional page ("golden ticket") in an artifact's back-stack upon a
flip interaction. Characteristics: (a) the roll is executed SERVER-side in
response to the flip event on a specific verified achievement record — never
client-side; (b) eligibility and pool selection derive from the record's
provenance (exemplarily: pools funded by the organizer whose event produced the
award, e.g., free entry to that organizer's next event; or sponsor pools scoped
by category/season); (c) the roll is seeded per (user, period) with rate
limits, so repeated flipping within a period deterministically reproduces the
same outcome and cannot farm rewards; (d) redemption for a minor participant is
routed to the guardian via a single-use, expiring redemption token delivered to
the guardian contact (reusing the claim-token pattern of Part II
infrastructure); (e) pool inventory, odds, pacing windows, and redemption
terms are operator-configured records (prize_pools; reveals logged in
prize_reveals with revealed_at/redeemed_at). In an editorial embodiment, the
same page mechanics deliver merit-selected (non-probabilistic) awards.

I.J. Guardian consent workflow for minor content (B2)
-----------------------------------------------------

[0026] Photo and text enrichment concerning a minor is accepted only from an
authenticated owner (participant owner, or studio owner of an actively
affiliated studio), only with an explicit recorded consent affirmation, and
only becomes publicly renderable at operator approval per section I.H. Upload
provenance (uploaded_by/created_by) is retained per item. This workflow,
combined with conditional materialization, means no surface — original or
derivative — can display minor-related content that lacks recorded consent and
approval.

PART II — GUARDIAN-MEDIATED SELECTIVE-DISCLOSURE PORTABLE IDENTITY
==================================================================

II.A. The portable identifier
-----------------------------

[0027] Each participant carries the lifetime identifier of paragraph [0013]:
globally unique, never recycled, never re-derived upon rename or record merge
(a stability contract), non-enumerable, and presentable by the participant at
any registration context ("her DancerID"). The identifier resolves within the
identity service to (a) a registration profile — an opt-in "registration
wallet" of fields the platform does not otherwise hold (date of birth, address,
guardian contacts, waiver signatures, emergency/medical fields), entered only
by the authenticated owner, never scraped, never public; and (b) verified
competitive-history attestations computed from the achievement-record corpus of
Part I (e.g., "N first-place solo results at level X in seasons Y–Z"),
usable by organizers for division placement and anti-sandbagging verification.

II.B. Consent grants (FIG. 10)
------------------------------

[0028] Disclosure is governed by grant records:

  id_grants(id, dancer_id, grantee_org_id, scope JSON, granted_by_user_id,
            expires_at, revoked_at, created_at)

where scope enumerates permitted fields (field-level granularity, e.g.
{"dob": true, "guardian_contact": true, "waivers": ["liability-2027"],
"history_attestations": true}), grantee_org_id names one specific organizer,
and expires_at bounds the grant in time. For a minor participant, only a
verified guardian account (the participant-profile owner) may create a grant;
grant creation captures a verifiable consent event (authenticated session plus
affirmative per-grant confirmation; embodiments add step-up verification
consistent with children's-privacy regulation). Grants are revocable at any
time (revoked_at); revocation and expiry take effect on the next access
attempt.

II.C. Organizer access (FIG. 11)
--------------------------------

[0029] An organizer accesses granted data via an authenticated pull API
(exemplarily GET /api/id/<unique_id>/profile) presenting an organizer API
token (HMAC-signed, single-purpose, expiring — following the platform's
existing signed-token patterns for claim links and unsubscribe links). The
service intersects the request with the grant scope and returns ONLY granted
fields; absent a live grant the response discloses nothing, including whether
the identifier exists. In a registration-prefill embodiment, the participant
initiates disclosure by presenting a consent link/QR at the organizer's
registration form, which redeems into a short-lived grant; the organizer's
form pre-fills from the response, eliminating re-keying and transcription
error.

II.D. Audit, revocation, minimization (FIG. 12)
-----------------------------------------------

[0030] Every disclosure is audit-logged (grant id, grantee, fields served,
timestamp, token identity); the guardian's console lists all grants and
disclosures with one-tap revocation. The service enforces data minimization
structurally: organizers integrate against the scope-filtered API rather than
receiving profile exports, so a grantee can never hold more than the granted
field set as of the last pull. Attestations of paragraph [0027](b) may be
served as computed claims (booleans/counts with provenance references) rather
than raw records, further minimizing disclosure while preserving verifiability.

II.E. Interoperation with Part I
--------------------------------

[0031] The identity service and the artifact system share the record corpus and
the identifier space: attestations derive from the same provenance-bearing
records that artifacts render; the guardian roles, ownership claims, consent
capture, moderation queues, and signed-token infrastructure of Part I are the
trust substrate of Part II. In embodiments, a redeemed reward of section I.I
routes through the Part II guardian-contact channel; an organizer that
integrates Part II registration prefill receives Part I colophon branding on
the artifacts its events produce, coupling the two inventions commercially and
technically.

ALTERNATIVE EMBODIMENTS (both parts)
------------------------------------

[0032] The dance domain is exemplary. Achievement records may be sports
results, academic honors, esports placements, certifications, or employment
credentials; "studio" generalizes to team/school/employer; "organizer" to any
credentialing or event-running entity. The artifact need not be a card:
certificates, badges, plaques, and 3D presentations may carry the same
conditionally-materialized page structure. Rendering may be client-side or
server-side; storage may use any relational or document store; moderation
tiers may use any rule set or classifier; the identifier may be embodied as a
QR code, NFC credential, or verifiable-credential (W3C VC) wrapper around the
same grant semantics; grants may be represented as signed capability tokens
carried by the participant rather than server-side rows. Numeric parameters
(280 characters, coin circularity, page order, grace thresholds) are
exemplary, not limiting.

CLAIMS (SAMPLE — provisional applications require none; included to frame scope)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. A system comprising: a datastore associating an achievement record imported
from a third-party source with one or more participant identities via junction
records, at least one junction record having associated content having a
moderation state; and a rendering engine configured to render the record as an
artifact having a front face and an ordered stack of back pages reachable
through a flip interaction, wherein each back page is materialized only when
its associated content exists and its moderation state satisfies an approval
condition, such that no rendered artifact contains an empty or unapproved page.

2. The system of claim 1, wherein a page renders per-participant annotation
content of a multi-participant record, the rendering engine composing the page
responsive to the identity of a viewing context by ordering the viewing
participant's annotation first.

3. The system of claim 1, wherein an editing mode renders the same artifact
with non-materialized pages materialized as inline-editable placeholders, and
wherein a public rendering mode never materializes said placeholders.

4. The system of claim 1, further comprising a brand-mark subsystem wherein a
third-party mark is rendered only within a fixed silhouette, geometric fitting
parameters of the mark are modifiable only by an operator role, appearance
parameters are modifiable by an owner role, and public display is conditioned
on an operator-set approval flag defaulting to disabled.

5. A method of generating derivative media for an achievement artifact,
comprising: enumerating the artifact's pages; evaluating, at generation time,
a moderation-state condition for each page's content; rasterizing only pages
satisfying the condition; and sequencing the rasterizations into a media asset
without human review of the asset, whereby the asset is publish-safe by
construction.

6. The method of claim 5, further comprising appending, as a terminal frame, a
composition derived from an organization branding record having an
operator-approval flag.

7. The method of claim 5, further comprising generating, for a
multi-participant record, a per-participant variant of the media asset by
reordering annotation content per participant.

8. A method comprising: receiving a flip interaction on an artifact bound to a
verified achievement record; executing, at a server, a probabilistic selection
over reward pools filtered by provenance metadata of the record; seeding the
selection per user and time period such that repeated interactions within the
period yield an identical outcome; and, when the selection yields a reward for
a minor participant, transmitting a single-use expiring redemption token to a
guardian contact associated with the participant.

9. A method of content propagation comprising: receiving enrichment content
for a first achievement record; identifying sibling records sharing an event
identifier and a performance identifier with the first record; attaching the
content to sibling records lacking content using insert-or-ignore semantics;
and propagating a subsequent moderation verdict on said content to all pending
identical copies from the same author.

10. An identity-disclosure method comprising: maintaining, for a participant, a
lifetime non-recycled public identifier and a profile comprising registration
fields and achievement attestations computed from provenance-bearing records;
receiving, from a verified guardian account, a grant record scoping a set of
permitted fields to one grantee organization with an expiry; receiving an
authenticated request presenting the identifier and a grantee credential;
intersecting the request with unexpired, unrevoked grant scope; returning only
fields within the intersection; and recording each disclosure in an audit log.

11. The method of claim 10, wherein absent a live grant the response does not
disclose existence of the identifier.

12. The method of claim 10, wherein the attestations are served as computed
claims referencing record provenance rather than as the underlying records.

13. The method of claim 10, further comprising revoking the grant upon guardian
action, the revocation effective at the next access attempt, and presenting the
guardian a log of all grants and disclosures.

14. The method of claim 10, wherein the grant is created by redemption of a
participant-presented consent link or code at a grantee registration surface,
the redeemed grant being short-lived and scoped to registration-prefill fields.

15. The system of claim 1, wherein participant identifiers in public resource
locators are non-enumerable lifetime identifiers, and numeric-key resource
locators return a not-found response without redirection.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[0033] An achievement record imported from third-party sources renders as a
flip-card artifact whose back is an ordered stack of pages — certificate,
photograph, acknowledgements, organizer colophon — each materialized only when
its content has passed a moderation gate, so every public rendering and every
automatically generated derivative (share image, short-form video) is
publish-safe by construction. Group awards compose per viewer from
junction-record annotations; owners edit the artifact itself in a WYSIWYG mode
whose placeholders never reach public surfaces; organizer branding enters only
through an operator-fitted, approval-gated coin; and a server-seeded,
provenance-scoped reward page may transiently appear with guardian-routed
redemption. A companion identity service gives each participant a lifetime
identifier through which a guardian creates field-scoped, expiring, revocable
disclosure grants; organizers pull only granted registration fields and
verified competitive-history attestations via authenticated, audit-logged API
calls, enabling error-free registration prefill and division verification for
minors without uncontrolled data sharing.
